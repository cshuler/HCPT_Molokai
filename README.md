# Hawaiʻi Cesspool Hazard Assessment & Prioritization Tool: Molokaʻi

This repository contains the data-processing workflows, scripts, maps, and outputs used to expand the **Hawaiʻi Cesspool Hazard Assessment & Prioritization Tool (HCPT)** to the island of **Molokaʻi**.

> **📄 [Download the Final Project Report](Refs/Molokai_HCPT_Final_Report.pdf)**

The Molokaʻi HCPT evaluates the relative environmental and human-health hazards associated with the island's approximately **1,280 documented cesspools**. The project extends the statewide HCPT framework to Molokaʻi using the same underlying methodology and 15 risk factors developed for the original Hawaiʻi assessment.

The work was conducted for the **Hawaiʻi Office of Planning and Sustainable Development, Coastal Zone Management Program**.

---

## Why this project was needed

Molokaʻi was not included in the original statewide HCPT because several critical datasets were unavailable, including a calibrated groundwater model, groundwater flowpaths, ecological risk layers, and a sufficiently complete cesspool inventory.

This project filled those gaps and integrated Molokaʻi into the statewide prioritization framework, allowing the island to be represented in future cesspool-conversion planning and funding decisions.

---

## Key Results

The analysis identified **1,280 cesspools** across Molokaʻi and evaluated their relative hazard at the census tract, block-group, and block scales.

Major findings include:

- **East Molokaʻi was classified as Priority 1** at the census-tract scale, while West Molokaʻi was Priority 3.
- The highest-priority block groups occur around **Kaunakakai**, where high cesspool density overlaps with shallow groundwater and sensitive coastal resources.
- At the census-block scale, **12 blocks containing 238 cesspools were classified as Priority 1**.
- The most important recurring risk drivers were **reef fishery value, coral reef value, shallow groundwater, cesspool density, and coastal wave exposure**.
- **Kaunakakai emerged as the clearest candidate for future sewer expansion** because of its high cesspool density, environmental vulnerability, and proximity to existing wastewater infrastructure.
- Block groups appear to provide a useful planning scale for Molokaʻi because they capture meaningful differences among the island's distinct cesspool settings.

## How the HCPT Works

Each cesspool is evaluated using **15 environmental, hydrologic, infrastructure, and coastal-resource risk factors**.

These include factors such as:

- distance to the coastline
- distance to streams and wetlands
- depth to groundwater
- drinking-water well proximity
- well capture zones
- sea-level-rise vulnerability
- soil characteristics
- cesspool density
- rainfall
- coral reef value
- reef fishery value
- coastal recreational use
- swimming beaches
- coastal exposure / wave power

A particularly important addition for Molokaʻi was the development of **groundwater flowpaths** using a MODFLOW groundwater model and MODPATH particle tracking. These flowpaths link individual cesspools to likely coastal groundwater-discharge areas, allowing coastal ecological and recreational risk factors to be associated with their potential upstream wastewater sources.

## Management Applications

The HCPT is intended as a **decision-support tool**, rather than a site-specific determination of wastewater impact.

Results can support:

- statewide cesspool-conversion prioritization
- identification of communities suitable for sewer expansion
- identification of areas where decentralized or advanced onsite treatment may be more appropriate
- evaluation of risks to groundwater and drinking-water resources
- identification of wastewater threats to coral reefs, fisheries, and coastal recreation
- targeting of future field investigations and infrastructure planning

For Molokaʻi, the analysis highlights four broadly different management settings:

1. high-density residential development around **Kaunakakai**
2. low-density coastal cesspools along the **south shore**
3. dispersed upland systems around **Hoʻolehua and Kualapuʻu**
4. resort-area systems near **Kepuhi Beach**

These areas have substantially different infrastructure needs and environmental risk profiles.

---

## Data and Limitations

The HCPT uses the best available statewide and Molokaʻi-specific datasets, but the results should be interpreted as a **relative prioritization framework**, not as direct measurements of contamination from individual cesspools.

The statewide cesspool inventory contains known uncertainties and will continue to evolve as improved wastewater-system information becomes available.

Some drinking-water well and well-capture-zone information is considered sensitive and is therefore not distributed in this public repository.

The analysis workflow has been designed so that updated cesspool inventories and environmental datasets can be incorporated as they become available.

---

## Related HCPT Work

This project is an extension of the statewide **Hawaiʻi Cesspool Hazard Assessment & Prioritization Tool**, originally developed to support Hawaiʻi's efforts to identify and prioritize cesspools for conversion.

The Molokaʻi expansion applies the same general scoring framework while developing the additional island-specific datasets necessary to represent Molokaʻi within the statewide system.
